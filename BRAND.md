# Puli: the brand

Notes to ourselves, not a page. This file is getpuli.com's brand guide; the company's, Deliberate Software, is `~/dev/deliberate-software.github.io/BRAND.md`. Where this file and the live site disagree, the site wins, and this file needs updating.

Copy is Alan's. Apply his line edits as he writes them, with the site's typography: curly quotes and apostrophes, the middle dot in eyebrows.

The app. Its site, getpuli.com, is the home page, How Lessons Work and a page per lesson, the Handler's Guide, Support, Privacy, Terms, Trademarks, Acknowledgements, and a 404.

## In one line

Your notes about the people you work with, the projects you work on, and the promises made along the way, filed for you, in the privacy of your own Mac.

## The name

Puli is named for a Hungarian herding dog with a corded coat. Say it POO-lee. A herding dog doesn't replace the shepherd: you decide what matters, and Puli makes sure none of it wanders off. Be subtle with the dog in words: herding, tricks, and lessons, not puns in every line. In pictures, she's the star.

## The home page

Every section has one job. If a line fits none of them, cut it.

1. **The hero.** Headline: "Herd your thoughts." Subhead: "Your notes about the people you work with, the projects you work on, and the promises made along the way, filed for you, in the privacy of your own Mac. For free. Forever." Then a real screenshot of the app.
2. **Why Puli.** "Thoughts are fleeting. Write them down." Transcripts only capture what was said; a note captures what you thought. "Preferably somewhere with good recall." A note about Dana and the Q3 launch belongs to both.
3. **How Puli Works.** "Just start writing. Puli takes it from there." The three pillars:
   - **People:** everyone you talk to has a page.
   - **Projects:** every project keeps its own thread.
   - **Promises:** every one in one place.
4. **Pricing.** "Free. Forever." Taking notes and capturing tasks are always free. Lessons teach Puli new tricks.
5. **Three promises.** We will never abuse your privacy. We will never steal your attention. We will never revoke your purchases.
6. **Questions,** then **About:** Alan's note, signed.
7. **The mailing list:** "Hear when Puli learns new tricks." Until launch, a plain "Coming soon to the Mac App Store." stands in for Apple's badge.

## The look

By day the page is pale teal, with light screenshots and a cream menu bar. By night it's ink teal, a near-black teal, with dark screenshots. Nothing is graphite at night.

- **Colors by day:** pale teal `#CFE1DD`, charcoal `#1F2023` text, muted `#44514E`, lines `#B1C9C4`. The app's amber `#8A4B08` is the one accent.
- **Colors by night:** the page `#10201F`, teal bands `#0A1615`, the menu bar and mailing list `#132826`, warm white `#FBF8F3` text, muted `#A9BFBB`, lines `#22383A` and `#2C4643`. Light amber `#FAC775` is the accent.
- **Menu bar:** the app editor's cream `#FBF9F4` with charcoal `#27282B` text by day; ink teal by night. It shows the icon, "Puli for Mac", and the links.
- **Tags:** pills in the app's colors. People are blue, organizations plum, projects green.
- **Type:** headings in IBM Plex Serif Light; text in Instrument Sans, the app's text face. On pages, h2 is 44px and h3 is Plex Serif Light at 26px, with no bold lead-ins.
- **No italics,** except Alan's About note on the home page, which reads like a quote.
- **Buttons:** the app's 8px corners.
- **Pages** other than the home page are dark teal at the top, with an "Updated" date eyebrow.

## The art

Painted illustrations, not photos, so the site feels like software: grass, poppies, and the Puli herself. The Puli is the star. The sheep were becoming the mascot by accident, so they moved to a far-off pasture (Nov 22).

- **The Puli** appears in the app icon and across the site, in poses from Alan's art: herding in the hero, standing, sniffing, lying, running in mid air in Questions, walking, and a downward dog on Thanks. Each pose does something that suits its section; she's never a logo pasted in. More poses come from Alan's art, in the same painted style, so she always looks like one dog.
- **The sheep** appear only far off: a painted pasture with the flock grazing in the distance, behind the meadow at the foot of the home page. Never a sheep up close, big enough to have a face.
- **The meadow:** grass and poppies throughout the home page. Every other page has a strip of meadow along its foot; about one page in five has a Puli there, and the 404 has the large sniffer, sniffing a tuft of grass.
- The art is always full color, by day and by night: never dim or filter it.
- The originals are in `tools/art/` (`pack/` for the Puli, `pasture/` for the flock, `meadow/` for the grass and poppies); the site's AVIFs are drawn from them with `tools/avif.swift`.

## Licensing and platform

- The basics, taking notes and managing tasks, are free, forever. Lessons are paid. Lessons you buy are yours to keep.
- Two plans, bought in the app through the App Store:
  - **A Year of Lessons:** every lesson so far, and every new one for 12 months. It renews yearly, starts with a month free, and can be cancelled anytime. Cancel and you keep every lesson you have; cancel during the free days and they lock again.
  - **Forever Home:** every lesson so far, and every one to come, bought once.
- Today, Triage, Progress, and Explore are free in Job Search journals, and the sample journals have every lesson.
- Prices live only in the App Store. Never put them on the site or in these notes, so a change is made in one place.
- Puli is Mac only, macOS 15 or later. Some features use Apple Intelligence, which needs a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later. Don't promise an iPhone app.

## Who it's for

They have three things in common:

1. **Many people, many projects, all at once.** A single note touches several of them.
2. **Time is the constraint.** They go from one conversation to the next, with no time to file anything.
3. **Promises go both ways.** They owe things, and they're owed things.

### Corporate managers and leaders

They run from meeting to meeting, managing several people and several projects. They have tasks of their own, and tasks they've handed to others. This is the primary audience, and the one the home page speaks to first.

Likely objection: "Can I use this at work?" Puli's privacy answers it: everything stays on the Mac, encrypted, and nothing is sent to us or anyone.

### Agency owners

They have clients, projects, and people, and time set aside to meet with each client or about them, plus time set aside for creative thinking.

### Entrepreneurs

They have ideas percolating into businesses, each in its own encrypted journal. They may have a giant list of contacts, and may not want it split across journals.

### Students and professors

They have classes, students, other professors, assignments, labs, and tests, with a journal for each semester or class.

### Job seekers

A job search is the manager's problem in its sharpest form: dozens of people and companies, conversations to prepare for, promises to keep, and high stakes. The Job Search sample journal shows it.

Job seekers become managers again. Someone who lands a job with Puli takes it to work.

**The corporate manager is who Puli is for. The job seeker is why Puli exists.**

## Who it's not for

If your notes belong somewhere else, or to someone else, Puli isn't for you.

- **Software engineers like Chris.** They have too few meetings, and their notes land in the repo, where others see them.
- **Real estate agents like Dara.** They live in MLS, so Puli would be a second place to keep the same people.
- **People writing long things,** like books, essays, or documentation.
- **Teams sharing a workspace.** Puli is private and single-person, for now.
- **Linked-note power users** who want backlinks, plugins, and a scriptable vault.
- **People who want AI to write for them.** Puli's AI reads your notes and points things out. It doesn't draft.
- **Anyone not on a Mac with macOS 15 or later.**

## Maybes, and what would make them a yes

- **Executives with a scribe or an AI recorder,** like Ryan. They'd be a yes if Puli could import a recording's summary or transcript and turn its action items into tasks.
- **Entrepreneurs with a giant list of contacts,** like Sandi. They'd be a yes if people could be shared across journals, maybe through Contacts. Today each journal keeps its own people, and Puli has no Contacts integration.

## The competition

| App | Headline | Then | The word it owns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agenda | Organize your days | Notes meets Calendar | days |
| Bear | Markdown notes you'll love | on Mac, iPhone, iPad | love |
| Obsidian | Sharpen your thinking | The free and flexible app for your private thoughts | thinking |
| Evernote | Your second brain | Remember everything and tackle any project with your notes, tasks, and schedule all in one place | everything |

Notion is for teams, not a competitor.

Obsidian already claims "private," so privacy alone doesn't set Puli apart. Nobody owns **people** or **promises**.

Evernote's feature list overlaps Puli's. Many of its users feel let down and are looking for somewhere to go. Puli doesn't import from Evernote yet.
